title: Documentation.AI Authentication
description: Let members of your organization sign in to your documentation with their Documentation.AI account.
---

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
  Documentation.AI authentication is available on all plans.
</Callout>

Readers sign in with the same Documentation.AI account they use for the dashboard. Every member of your organization can read your protected documentation, whatever their role: **Admin**, **Editor** or **Viewer**. There is no password to share and no identity provider to configure.

It suits internal documentation for a team that already works in Documentation.AI. You decide who can read by managing your organization's members.

Like [Password](/customize/access-control/password), it gives every signed-in reader all protected pages. It doesn't support [role-based access control](/customize/access-control/overview#role-based-access-control) or per-user content. For those, use [JWT](/customize/access-control/jwt) or [OAuth 2.0](/customize/access-control/oauth).

Documentation.AI authentication works in both modes:

- **Private**: All pages require sign-in.
- **Partial**: A mix of public and protected pages. You choose which pages are public with the `public` property in your navigation, as described in [Partial access control](/customize/access-control/password#partial-access-control).

## Set up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose access mode" icon="lock" title-type="p">
    In your dashboard, go to **Settings > Access Control** and select **Private** (all pages protected) or **Partial** (mix of public and protected pages).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select the Documentation.AI method" icon="key" title-type="p">
    In the **Authentication Method** selector, choose **Documentation.AI**, then click **Save**.

    There is nothing else to configure. Sign-in works on your Documentation.AI subdomain, your custom domain and subpath deployments, with no callback URL to register.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Re-publish" icon="upload" title-type="p">
    Re-publish your documentation so the deployed site uses the updated configuration.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add your readers" icon="users" title-type="p">
    In **Settings > Members**, add each person who should read the documentation to your organization. The **Viewer** role is enough to read.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How readers sign in

1. A reader opens a protected page.
2. They're taken to the Documentation.AI sign-in page. If they're already signed in to the dashboard in the same browser, this step is skipped.
3. Documentation.AI checks that they're a member of your organization.
4. They return to the page they opened, signed in.

If the account isn't a member of your organization, the reader sees **You don't have access** with the email they signed in with. They can ask one of your admins to add them, or click **Use different account** to sign in with another account.

## Remove access

Remove the person from your organization in **Settings > Members**. Access is checked again every 5 minutes while someone reads, so they lose access to pages, search, the AI Assistant and the [Reader MCP Server](/ai/reader-mcp-server) within 5 minutes.

## Sessions

- Readers stay signed in for **14 days**. After that, they're asked to sign in again. If they're still signed in to the dashboard, this happens without a prompt.
- Signing out of the dashboard doesn't sign them out of the documentation. To end someone's access, remove them from your organization.

## AI tools and embedded docs

- **AI Assistant and search** work for signed-in readers, with no extra setup.
- **Reader MCP Server**: Connect to the `/_mcp/auth` endpoint and sign in with your Documentation.AI account during the OAuth flow. If you're removed from the organization, the client loses access within 5 minutes. See [Reader MCP Server](/ai/reader-mcp-server#authentication-for-private-documentation).
- **Embedded docs**: Your server signs users in with reader tokens. Documentation.AI doesn't check organization membership for these users; your server decides who gets a token. See [Private Docs](/embedded-docs/private-docs).

## Example

Your team keeps its engineering handbook at `handbook.example.com`. Everyone who should read it already has a Documentation.AI account. Choose **Private** mode with **Documentation.AI** authentication. Then add new joiners as **Viewers** in **Settings > Members**, and remove people when they leave.
